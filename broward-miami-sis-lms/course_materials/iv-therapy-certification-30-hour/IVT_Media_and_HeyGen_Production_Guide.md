# BMHI IV Therapy Media and HeyGen Production Guide

## Recommended production approach

Use the instructor scripts embedded in the speaker notes of every PowerPoint slide. Import each module into HeyGen, select one professional nursing instructor avatar, and paste the matching slide script into the scene narration. Use captions and a calm instructional pace. Keep demonstrations clearly labeled as simulation or task-trainer practice.

## Lowest-cost media sources

1. **Pexels first:** Free photos and videos for commercial use. Attribution is not required, but record the creator and source URL for BMHI's production file. https://www.pexels.com/license/
2. **Pixabay second:** Free stock photos and videos under the Pixabay Content License. Keep the original asset URL and download record. https://pixabay.com/service/license-summary/

Do not use an image merely because it looks medical. A qualified nurse should review every selected asset for correct equipment, PPE, insertion technique, tubing setup, labeling, and patient positioning before publication.

## HeyGen production plan

### Course-wide settings

- Create one HeyGen project for each module. Keep the PowerPoint slide order unchanged.
- Use one professional nursing-instructor avatar throughout the course. Place the avatar on the left or right according to the open space on the slide.
- Use a calm instructional voice at approximately 0.95 to 1.0 playback speed. Pronounce “IV” as “I-V” and “IVPB” as “I-V piggyback.”
- Import the PowerPoint, then copy the `HEYGEN INSTRUCTOR SCRIPT` from each slide’s speaker notes into the matching scene.
- Use closed captions. Check all medical terms, abbreviations, drug names, units, and formulas manually after automatic captioning.
- Use a brief crossfade between slides. Avoid animated text, dramatic music, and rapid camera movement.
- Keep the instructor visible during explanations. Hide or minimize the avatar when the learner needs to read a calculation, chart, or checklist.
- Label every task-trainer sequence `Simulation demonstration`. Do not present stock footage as proof of correct clinical technique.

### Video deliverables

1. Export each module as a separate 1080p MP4.
2. Use filenames `IVT_Module_01_HeyGen.mp4` through `IVT_Module_08_HeyGen.mp4`.
3. Produce caption files in WebVTT format for the student portal.
4. Keep the PowerPoint available beside the video as an accessible learner download.
5. Record the HeyGen avatar, voice, export date, asset sources, and nurse-review approval in the production log.

### Final clinical review

Before publishing, a qualified nurse must review the complete exported video rather than only the PowerPoint. The reviewer should verify equipment, PPE, device terminology, calculations, insertion and dressing technique, patient positioning, narration, captions, and the distinction between demonstration and supervised competency evaluation.

## Supplemental peripheral-IV source integration

BMHI incorporated selected instructional material from *Peripheral Intravenous Initiation Self Learning Module*, FH Vascular Access Regional Shared Work Team, Version 5, May 2012, into the current PowerPoint series. The additions cover patient comfort and positioning, vein-wall anatomy, palpation, vein-distension strategies, unsuccessful-insertion response, infusion-flow troubleshooting, competency decision criteria, and catheter-integrity assessment during removal.

The source originated outside Florida and contains dated facility-specific practices. BMHI did not import its jurisdiction-specific scope rules, branded pump procedures, fixed tubing-change intervals, or superseded facility protocols. Instructors must teach the adapted slides using current Florida requirements, current manufacturer instructions, and the learner’s clinical-site policy.

## Additional source integration for versions 13 and 14

BMHI also reviewed *30 Hour IV Certification Study Guide* (revised March 2013) and *Phlebotomy Skills and Practicum Review*. Version 13 adds student-friendly teaching slides on vein-versus-artery recognition, sharps injury prevention, immediate occupational-exposure response, vascular-access device distinctions, and urgent central-line findings. Each added slide has an original HeyGen narration script in its speaker notes and identifies the supporting source pages.

Version 14 expands Module 3 with readable electrolyte tables instead of reproducing the packet's crowded multi-page chart. Separate slides cover common adult reference ranges, functions, sodium, potassium, calcium, magnesium, phosphorus, chloride, and a structured nursing safety check. The slides emphasize assessment, verification, IV-related risks, and escalation. They do not prescribe independent treatment.

## Comprehensive visual revision for version 15

Version 15 reviews the complete 78-page *30 Hour IV Certification Study Guide* and distributes its clinically relevant teaching content across all eight modules. The expanded material covers IV purposes and risks, patient assessment and education, fluid movement and third spacing, solution categories, medication calculations, compatibility, blood components, transfusion monitoring, parenteral nutrition, chemotherapy, skin and vascular anatomy, vein selection, administration equipment, delivery methods, peripheral and central complications, midlines, PICCs, tunneled and nontunneled central catheters, implanted ports, arterial lines, hypodermoclysis, documentation, terminology, and competency checkoffs.

The PowerPoints do not reproduce the study guide's scanned pages. Dense source tables and long paragraphs have been converted into editable comparison tables, process slides, clinical decision prompts, and speaker-note scripts. Three original BMHI educational visuals were added: forearm vein-and-artery anatomy, generic IV bags with tubing and an infusion pump, and a vascular-access-device comparison. These visuals were generated for this course and contain no patient identifiers, brand names, or medication labels.

The 2013 packet remains a historical content source. Version 15 excludes or reframes outdated Florida rule text, the former 18-hour/12-hour schedule, obsolete product names, fixed tubing and dressing intervals, generic flush recipes, historical infection statistics, and medication-treatment lists that require current orders. A qualified Florida RN must complete the final clinical and regulatory review before publication.

## Teaching-flow and visual revision for version 16

Version 16 responds to faculty review that the decks needed a stronger instructor-led sequence and more visual variety. Module 1 now teaches all seven medication and fluid rights and connects them to ongoing monitoring. Module 3 adds a visual fluid-compartment lesson, physiologic regulation of water balance, current crystalloid examples, fluid-selection questions, and a before-during-after reassessment sequence. Module 7 now shows an actual skin cross-section, explains why every skin layer matters, maps the superficial veins of the hand and forearm, compares advantages and disadvantages of common hand, wrist, forearm, and antecubital sites, and presents a photographed IV-start supply setup with a complete checklist.

Four original BMHI course visuals were added for this revision: skin and subcutaneous anatomy, the upper-extremity superficial venous network, an organized peripheral-IV supply tray, and a three-panel fluid-compartment and tonicity illustration. The standard slide layout also received stronger top and bottom color framing while preserving large readable type, editable tables, source notes, and the HeyGen script on every slide.

The older study guide was used as a topic source, not as current regulatory or procedural authority. BMHI excluded its former 18-hour/12-hour schedule, outdated Florida-rule wording, historical infection statistics, fixed flush recipes, legacy dressing and tubing intervals, and device-brand instructions. The phlebotomy packet's certification fees, order-of-draw content, specimen-processing material, and phlebotomy-only procedures were also excluded. Current Florida law, OSHA requirements, manufacturer instructions, and facility policy control whenever they differ from the source packets.
