SLIDE 19
Let us review the major takeaways from Chapter 21. The lymphatic system drains tissue fluid and houses the immune system. Innate immunity provides fast, general defense; adaptive immunity is specific and remembers. Inflammation brings defenses to the site of injury or infection. T cells coordinate the response and kill infected cells; B cells make antibodies. Immune memory underlies long-term protection and the success of vaccines. Before moving on, pause and explain each point without reading the slide. If any statement is difficult to explain, mark that topic for review before the quiz.

SLIDE 20
This final slide connects Chapter 21 to practical nursing. Vaccination. Builds immune memory without causing the disease. Allergies. An overactive immune response to harmless substances. Autoimmune Disease. The immune system attacks the body's own tissues. HIV/AIDS. The virus destroys helper T cells, crippling immunity. Lymph Node Exam. Swollen nodes can signal infection or, sometimes, cancer. Immunocompromise. Weakened immunity raises infection risk in many patients. Your role is to observe carefully, protect safety, follow orders and precautions, document objectively, and report changes through the appropriate chain of command. Use these examples to connect anatomy with the care you will provide.
